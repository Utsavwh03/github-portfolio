Research

I'm interested in reinforcement learning as the route to agents that keep getting better from their own experience, whether that agent is a language model, a robot, or something in between. My work spans the methods that make this possible: how agents are trained after pretraining, how they explore, how they adapt to new tasks, and how they keep learning without forgetting.

RL and post-training for LLMs

Post-training approaches such as RLHF, preference optimization, and RL with verifiable rewards; RL pretraining objectives; and using RL to improve reasoning and tool-using agents.

Continual and meta-learning

Agents that adapt quickly and keep learning over time: meta-learning and in-context RL, plasticity and forgetting, and consolidating what is learned at test time into model weights.

Deep RL for robotics and world models

Learning control from interaction, world models that let agents plan and practice in imagination, and bringing large pretrained models into embodied decision-making.

Exploration and scaling RL

Scaling RL toward generalist agents: exploration guided by foundation-model priors, unsupervised and self-supervised RL objectives, and the representations and value functions that let RL scale.

Across all of these, I care about building self-improving agents that stay safe and aligned as they learn.

Publications

Hierarchical Experimentalist Agents

Abhranil Chandra, Utsav Dhanuka, S. Vaidyanathan, V. Gandhi, Scott Niekum

  • RL Beyond Rewards Workshop, RLC 2026
  • PTA Workshop, NeurIPS 2026
  • Meta Agents Workshop, NeurIPS 2026
  • Under review, ICLR 2027

HExA is an in-context RL framework in which a frozen LLM learns through active experimentation. An actor designs experiments, an evolver distills successful and failed trajectories into a skill bank of strategies and recurring mistakes, and a retriever feeds relevant skills into future episodes. No weights change. We also introduce Interphyre, a benchmark of interactive physics tasks where frontier models score 0% zero-shot.
