Research

Foundation models know a lot about the world, but knowing the laws of physics doesn't solve an experimental physics problem. In new environments, the right strategy has to be discovered through interaction. I study what makes that kind of learning efficient and scalable.

Learning from experimentation

Agents that design experiments, test hypotheses, and distill interaction experience into reusable, composable skills that transfer across tasks.

From in-context to parametric learning

Consolidating skills discovered at test time into model weights through RL post-training and continual learning, so agents keep improving without forgetting.

Exploration with foundation-model priors

Using what pretrained models already know to guide exploration in complex environments, together with unsupervised RL objectives that don't depend on hand-designed rewards.

More broadly, I'm interested in scaling RL for generalist agents across language, embodied, and robotic settings, and in keeping self-improving agents safe and aligned.

Publications

Hierarchical Experimentalist Agents

Abhranil Chandra, Utsav Dhanuka, S. Vaidyanathan, V. Gandhi, Scott Niekum

  • RL Beyond Rewards Workshop, RLC 2026
  • PTA Workshop, NeurIPS 2026
  • Meta Agents Workshop, NeurIPS 2026
  • Under review, ICLR 2027

HExA is an in-context RL framework in which a frozen LLM learns through active experimentation. An actor designs experiments, an evolver distills successful and failed trajectories into a skill bank of strategies and recurring mistakes, and a retriever feeds relevant skills into future episodes. No weights change. We also introduce Interphyre, a benchmark of interactive physics tasks where frontier models score 0% zero-shot.
